For law firms

What a client sees
before they call you

Two people decide whether you get the case. One is the search engine, deciding whether your page is worth showing at all. The other is the person who has just been in an accident, reading on a phone at eleven at night. We read your pages as both, and we send back the gap in numbers — not opinions.

What we actually look at

The line under your name in a search result
Every attorney page and every practice-area page. If there is no description, the search engine writes one — usually just the firm name and the city. We list each page where that happens, and what it currently says instead.
Whether the page answers the question
Not "do we rank". Whether a person who typed a specific situation finds a page that speaks to that situation, with a name attached to it and a way to call. Thick enough to be useful, short enough to read on a phone.
Whether the page can be quoted
Names, practice areas, office locations, phone numbers. Marked up properly, they can be lifted into results. Not marked up, they exist only for a human reader who already found you.

What you get back

  • A row per page: what is missing, what it currently shows instead, and what it should say.
  • Written in your words, not ours. If a sentence would not survive a partner reading it, it does not go in.
  • The pages ranked by how much they cost you — not alphabetically, not by page count.

What we do not do

  • We do not touch your intake forms, your client communications or anything a client sends you.
  • We do not publish anything to your site. You get a document; you decide what goes on.
  • We do not promise rankings, case volume or outcomes. Nobody honest does.
  • We do not add you to a list. One email, one reply, and it ends there if you want it to.

Send us one URL

One page — an attorney page, a practice area, whatever you would fix first. We come back with the gap in numbers. No charge, no signup, no call required.
